## Background
As a **UX Engineer**, we are directly integrated into the **design team** structure to collaborate closely with designers every day. This closeness previously led to the creation of the mature and widely adopted [BLUE Web Design System](/projects/blue-web-design-system) across all *tribes*, even achieving a score of <strong className="text-primary font-semibold">8.48</strong> in reducing redundancy and <strong className="text-primary font-semibold">8.42</strong> in accelerating delivery during the mid-2024 survey.

However, the condition of the mobile design system was very different because its development was handled by a developer team outside the design team structure, focusing more on feature development. As a result, <strong className="text-primary font-semibold">the mobile design system lagged behind the web</strong> and was still using the old standard (**BLUE 2.0**), with an inconsistent design language between the *Customer* and *Seller* ecosystems. Meanwhile, more than 85% of Blibli users access the application via mobile devices.

Based on these conditions, we initiated the development of the <strong className="text-primary font-semibold">BLUE Android Design System</strong> to align visual and technical standards across the Blibli application ecosystem through collaboration between UX Engineers, designers, and Android developers.

## Problem Identification

Based on this background, there were several main problems in the development of the mobile design system, namely:

* <strong className="text-primary font-semibold">The mobile design system lagged behind the web</strong> because it lacked focused management.
* **The use of the old design standard (BLUE 2.0)** caused inconsistencies in the design language across platforms and services.
* The fragmented maintenance process created redundancies and hindered team work efficiency.
* The absence of a single standard on the mobile platform led to inconsistent user experience quality.

## Goals & Objectives

The main goal of this project was to build the <strong className="text-primary font-semibold">BLUE Android Design System</strong> as a single standard aligning the design language and technical implementation across the Blibli application ecosystem.

To achieve this goal, several key objectives were established as follows:

* <strong className="text-primary font-semibold">Equalizing the quality of the mobile design system with the web</strong> by strengthening cross-role collaboration (UX Engineers, designers, and Android developers).
* Unifying the Customer and Seller ecosystems into the **BLUE 3.0** standard to replace the old standard (**BLUE 2.0**).
* Building a centralized design system as a *Single Source of Truth* (SSOT) to reduce redundancy and fragmentation in the maintenance process.
* Aligning the cross-platform design language to ensure a consistent user experience throughout the Blibli application ecosystem.

## Roles & Responsibilities

In this project, I acted as part of the **UX Engineer** team, collaborating directly with designers and Android developers, with responsibilities including:

* Developing BLUE Android components that are *reusable*, *scalable*, and consistent in implementation.
* Establishing the foundation of design tokens such as colors, typography, sizing, opacity, and elevation to maintain visual consistency.
* Developing an *icon library* to reduce asset redundancy and maintain visual consistency.
* Ensuring the quality and consistency of the design system through technical documentation, *code reviews*, *testing*, and periodic evaluations.
* Supporting cross-platform design system synchronization to ensure a consistent user experience across the Blibli application ecosystem.

### 3. Development & Implementation

The development and implementation of the BLUE Android Design System were carried out iteratively through several sprints using a collaborative and continuous approach. The entire process ran in parallel and continuously evolved to meet the needs of each sprint.

* The initial phase focused on migrating the *design token script* and *component library* to a new repository as the main foundation of the design system.
* An audit was conducted on the migrated code and components to map their compliance with the BLUE design system standards and to identify areas that needed fixing or refinement.
* Implementing *improvements* based on the audit results by adjusting token values and refining components from both visual and code perspectives.
* Developing new components iteratively to complete the *library*'s needs, alongside the *bug fixing* process and standard adjustments.
* The team also initiated an *icon library* as a new icon system on the Android platform to fulfill the need for previously unavailable visual assets.
* All development outputs were required to go through a cross-team *code review* and *Design QA* before being implemented into production.
* The team also built a CI/CD pipeline together with the R&D team to automate the *build* process and distribution of the Android Design System *library*.
* The *library release* process was handled by the UX Engineer team via the CI/CD pipeline, distributing artifacts to the Google Artifact Registry (GAR), accompanied by creating *release notes* and *release announcements* to ease adoption by the developer team.
* Each sprint concluded with a *retrospective* session to evaluate the workflow and improve the effectiveness of team collaboration.


## Results
After approximately six months of development, the first version of the BLUE Android Design System was successfully released in July 2024. This release introduced three main foundations: the Component Library, Design Tokens, and Icon Library, supporting the standardization of Android development in a more consistent, modular, and centralized manner for all products within the Blibli application ecosystem.

### Design Tokens
*Design Tokens* act as the *single source of truth* for foundational visual elements like colors, typography, sizing, spacing, and elevation. The token structure in BLUE Android adopts a tiered architecture consisting of:

* ***Global Tokens***, which store fundamental values (raw values) such as base colors and constant size numbers.
* ***Alias Tokens***, which assign specific meaning or purpose to global values, acting as the primary bridge for theming needs like automatically switching between light mode and dark mode.
* ***Component Tokens***, which bind specific values directly to elements in the component library so that global changes don't break the structure of other components.

### Icon Library
The Icon Library is a dedicated repository acting as the central hub for managing all icon assets modularly on the Android platform. Through this initiative, the entire iconography system is distributed through a single source as optimized Vector Drawables integrated directly into the Jetpack Compose code.

This system simplifies consistent icon usage for developers, eliminates the manual process of importing detached XML asset files that are prone to duplication, and significantly helps cut down the final application installation file size (APK size optimization).

## Impact

The implementation and management of the BLUE Android Design System have had a positive impact on both product development efficiency and professional competence development.

### Impact on the Company

* Visual consistency and product interactions have improved through the standardization of the BLUE Android Design System.
* The Android feature *delivery* process has become faster by leveraging ready-to-use components from the centralized *library*.
* Reduced asset duplication through the implementation of a centralized icon library, eliminating the need for extra scripts previously used by Android developers to detect duplicate assets.
* Supported the Blibli *rebranding* process on the Android platform through a more consistent and standardized design system implementation.
* Cross-team collaboration among UX Engineers, Android CE, Android SE, and Product Designers became more focused through a standardized workflow.
* Independent repository management under the UX Engineer team helped reduce inter-team dependencies and simplified the long-term maintenance of UI assets.
* The library distribution process became more efficient by implementing CI/CD pipelines and standardized *release pipelines*.

### Personal Impact

* Enhanced technical understanding regarding the implementation of *design tokens* and *component libraries* based on Jetpack Compose on the Android platform.
* Sharpened skills in building and managing large-scale design systems, from the initiation phase to code quality standardization.
* Strengthened experience in *technical governance* through cross-team *code review* processes and *Design QA* coordination.
* Honed analytical abilities in conducting code audits and structurally refining components.
* Provided new experiences in managing *library releases*, *CI/CD pipelines*, and distributing artifacts to the Google Artifact Registry (GAR).
